The clock inside the Lighthouse of Oakhaven was not powered by gears, but by the memory of tides. Every evening, an old keeper named Arthur climbed the winding stairs to polish its brass face. He had outlived his shipmates, his family, and the village at the base of the cliff, which had long since vanished into the shifting sands.Arthur’s only companion was a silver pocket watch that stayed silent, its hands permanently frozen at twelve o'clock.One stormy night, a sound broke through the howling wind—not of waves, but of a heavy iron key turning inside the lighthouse door. Arthur hurried down the steps, his lantern casting long, nervous shadows on the stone walls. Standing in the doorway was a young girl, no older than ten, soaked to the bone and shivering. She held nothing but a small leather-bound journal.
